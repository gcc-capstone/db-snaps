# COMP 451
# Cognitive Walkthrough

This assignment is used to assess ABET Student Outcome 3 for this course.

## Overview

This is an individual assignment. Each person on your team should complete a cognitive walkthrough for one representative task. You should try to choose a different representative task to analyze than your teammates.

If your project does not have enough appropriate tasks for every team member to choose a different one, multiple people may analyze the same task, but the work must be completed independently.

An important goal of this assignment is to identify ways your UI could be improved, so choose a task that is non-trivial or confusing to complete. The task should involve several meaningful user actions or decisions.

Do not choose a trivial task such as following a single obvious link.

Conduct the walkthrough using your team's current UI prototype.

## Technical Writing and Figures

Write this document as concise technical documentation rather than as an essay.

Use headings, short paragraphs, tables, or a combination of these to make the walkthrough easy to follow.

Include screenshots of the interface where they help the reader understand the task or your analysis. Most walkthroughs will probably need about 2–5 figures rather than a screenshot of every step.

For each figure:

- number the figure
- place a short descriptive caption below it
- refer to the figure by number in the text

Example caption:

Figure 2. Project creation form after the user enters an invalid date.

Example reference in the text:

The user next attempts to submit the form shown in Figure 2.

Crop screenshots when appropriate so that the important part of the interface is easy to see.

Your explanations and analysis should use clear, complete sentences. Concise phrases are appropriate in tables when their meaning is clear.

If you have already changed part of your prototype as a result of earlier UI work, you may include before-and-after figures when they help explain the problem and your improvement. Before-and-after figures are optional, but they often make your decisions and analysis more clear, and you will be graded partly on clarity.


You are encouraged to have another person review your draft for clarity before submitting it.

## Required Content

### 1. Representative Task — 2%

Clearly state the representative task you are analyzing.

### 2. Hypothetical User — 8%

Describe the hypothetical user performing the task in no more than 2–3 sentences.

You do not need to create a full persona. Include only characteristics that could affect the walkthrough, such as:

- familiarity with the application
- familiarity with the problem domain
- technical experience
- relevant prior knowledge
- other characteristics that affect what the user is likely to notice, understand, remember, or expect

Your analysis should be based on this user, not on what you or your development team already know about the interface.

### 3. Cognitive Walkthrough — 40%

Walk through the task from beginning to end.

For each meaningful step, describe:

- what the user is trying to accomplish
- the action the user needs to take
- what the user sees or knows that could lead them to that action
- what the user must notice, understand, or remember
- what feedback the interface provides after the action

Focus on meaningful user actions and decisions. You do not need to document minor physical actions such as moving the mouse or scrolling unless they are important to the usability of the task.

The goal is not simply to describe what the interface does. You are evaluating the task from the perspective of your hypothetical user.



### 4. Analysis — 30%

Analyze the important findings from your walkthrough.

Use the design principles discussed in class to explain why parts of the interface are likely to work well or cause difficulty. Relevant principles may include:

- visibility
- clutter
- grouping
- consistency
- conventions
- feedback
- cognitive load
- error prevention and recovery

You do not need to discuss every principle. Apply the principles that are relevant to the task and interface you analyzed.

Identify both:

- parts of the task that are likely to work well, and
- places where the user could become confused, choose the wrong action, fail to notice something important, have to remember unnecessary information, misunderstand the feedback, or otherwise have difficulty completing the task.

Explain your reasoning. Do not simply state that something is "good," "bad," "clear," or "confusing."

For example, rather than writing:

> The Create button is easy to use.

explain why:

> The Create button is visually prominent and uses terminology consistent with the rest of the application, so the user is likely to recognize it as the next action.

You do not need to write a lengthy analysis of every obvious step. Focus on findings that reveal something meaningful about the interface.

Refer to figures when they help support your analysis.

### 5. UI Improvement — 20%

Propose at least one specific improvement to the UI based on your walkthrough and analysis.

Your improvement may:

- address a usability problem identified during the walkthrough
- make an important action easier to discover
- reduce clutter or cognitive load
- make system feedback clearer
- prevent an error or make recovery easier
- improve consistency with the rest of the application or with familiar UI conventions
- eliminate an unnecessary step
- otherwise make the task easier or more efficient to complete

Explain which finding from your walkthrough motivates the change and which design principle or principles support your decision.

Be specific about what should change and what the revised UI should do or look like.

Do not write only:

> The navigation should be improved.

Instead, describe the actual change you would make and explain why it should improve the user's experience.

If you have already made the improvement in your prototype, you may use before-and-after figures to show the change. This is encouraged when the images communicate the improvement more clearly than a lengthy written description, but it is not required.


## Submission

Submit either:

- a Microsoft Word document (`.docx`), or
- a Markdown file (`.md`).

Include the necessary UI figures in or with your submission so that the document can be understood without opening your mock-ui separately.

## AI and Editing

The cognitive walkthrough, analysis, and proposed UI improvement should represent your own reasoning.

Before using AI or another person for editing help, complete a rough draft of your walkthrough and analysis yourself.  You must submit this rough draft to show your original thoughts, but the rough draft will not be graded or examined for proper grammar, sentence style, etc.

After you have developed your own ideas, you may use AI tools, classmates, the Writing Center, or other resources to help improve:

- grammar
- clarity
- conciseness
- organization
- technical writing

Do not use AI to generate the walkthrough reasoning, identify the usability problems for you, or decide what UI improvement you should propose.

You are responsible for the final document and should be able to explain the reasoning behind everything you submit.
